## Detailed Analysis of Current State and Prior Findings

In Phase 8, we successfully implemented an unsupervised spatial bottleneck (minimizing soft spatial variance) combined with closed-loop active probing. This effort successfully addressed the "Spatial Specialization Gap" identified in Phase 7 (where representation coordinates were smeared across multiple dimensions). 

Our previous findings established a critical trade-off:
- **No regularization (Control)** allows high-capacity representation but leads to high spatial variance (1209.33) and a 40.0% representation collapse rate.
- **Gentle regularization ($\lambda=0.01$)** reduces coordinate decoding MSE to 69.11 (an improvement over Control's 83.12) and prevents collapse (0.0% collapse rate), but does not achieve tight localization.
- **Strong regularization ($\lambda=0.1$)** successfully forces tight spatial localization (60.29 spatial variance, a 95% reduction), but restricts the predictive representation capacity too severely, degrading decoding MSE to 106.87 and restricting the average absolute Pearson correlation to 0.2907.

The open question is whether a **surprise-modulated adaptive curriculum** can resolve this trade-off. Under this curriculum, $\lambda(t)$ starts at $0$ (allowing the network to first acquire predictive features without spatial restriction) and dynamically scales up to $\lambda_{max} = 0.10$ as the temporal prediction error/surprise drops (forcing the stable representation to compress into a tightly localized spotlight).
