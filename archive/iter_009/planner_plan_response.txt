## Phase 9 Research Plan: Dynamic Surprise-Modulated Spatial Bottleneck Curriculum (DSMC)

### 1. Context and Motivation
In Phase 8, we explored an unsupervised spatial localization bottleneck coupled with active probing in our "Thalamus" network. We uncovered a critical trade-off:
- **Strong Bottlenecks ($\lambda \ge 0.1$)** successfully force highly localized spatial activation (reducing soft spatial variance by over 95%), but restrict representational capacity too severely, degrading centroid decoding MSE (from $83.12$ to $106.87$).
- **Gentle Bottlenecks ($\lambda = 0.01$)** achieve excellent predictive capacity (centroid decoding MSE of $69.11$) and complete collapse prevention (0.0% collapse rate), but fail to enforce tight spatial localization.

This trade-off occurs because a static bottleneck applies heavy regularization from the very beginning of training, when the network has not yet learned the fundamental dynamics of the environment, thereby blocking capacity when it is needed most. 

### 2. The Proposed Solution: DSMC
Rather than using a static penalty, we propose a **Dynamic Surprise-Modulated Spatial Bottleneck Curriculum (DSMC)**. This approach dynamically scales the bottleneck coefficient $\lambda_t$ as an inverse function of the local prediction surprise:
- When surprise $\bar{S}_t$ is high (e.g., early in training, or immediately after a 4th object is introduced), the bottleneck is relaxed ($\lambda_t \to 0$), giving the network the full freedom of its representational space to capture the new dynamics.
- As surprise decays (indicating that the temporal-prediction model has mastered the object’s dynamics), the bottleneck is tightened ($\lambda_t \to \lambda_{max}$), squeezing the learned features onto a localized spatial coordinate without losing predictive accuracy.

This dynamic curriculum aims to achieve the "best of both worlds": the tight spatial localization of a strong bottleneck and the high predictive accuracy of a gentle bottleneck.
