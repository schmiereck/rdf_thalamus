### **3. Scientific Analysis of the Findings**

#### **A. Why did Gradient-Driven Active Subspace Recruitment (GDASR) fail to trigger?**
The failure to recruit a 3rd dimension reveals an important edge-case in our original mathematical design of the GDASR trigger threshold. Let's look at the recruitment trigger:
$$E_{\text{EMA}} > \text{mean}(E_{\text{buffer}}) + k \cdot \text{std}(E_{\text{buffer}})$$
where $E_{\text{buffer}}$ stores the historical values of the error $E_{\text{EMA}}$ during the stable $d_t=2$ training.

1. **Inflated Baseline Mean and Standard Deviation:** 
   Our implementation appended *all* historical error values during $d_t=2$ (starting from Step 1) to `error_buffer`. Because the network starts with randomly initialized weights, the prediction errors at the beginning of training (Steps 1–200) were extremely high ($>0.15$). 
   By keeping these early high-error steps in the buffer, the historical `mean` and `std` of the buffer became **heavily inflated**.
2. **Modest Error Spike during Transition:** 
   When the environment transitioned to $N=3$ at Step 1500, the actual prediction error only spiked to about **$0.032$** (because the encoder already had a reasonable feature-extraction capability from training on $N=2$).
3. **The Result:** 
   Because the baseline threshold `mean + 4 * std` was inflated by the early training phase (e.g., thresholding at $>0.12$), the transition spike of $0.032$ was not high enough to break through the threshold. Thus, recruitment never occurred.

#### **B. Why did Representation Collapse occur?**
The extremely high mean absolute correlation ($r > 0.99$ for $d_t=2$) is a clear sign of severe representation collapse:
1. **VICReg Loss Weight Balance:** 
   The loss is weighted as:
   $$\mathcal{L} = 25.0 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{sim}} + 25.0 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{var}} + 1.0 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}}$$
   The covariance regularizer $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}}$ was weighted at $1.0$, which is relatively weak compared to the variance and similarity losses weighted at $25.0$. 
2. **Early-Stage Collapse:** 
   During the first few hundred steps, the encoder prioritized minimizing similarity loss by mapping inputs to a small, low-dimensional manifold. Once the representations became collinear, the weak covariance regularizer ($1.0$) was unable to force the dimensions to separate.

---

### **4. Recommended Next Steps for the Research Project**

To address these findings and refine the Thalamus architecture in subsequent iterations, we should:
1. **Reset or Window the Error Buffer:** Change the `error_buffer` to a rolling window (e.g., only keep the last 200 or 500 steps) or completely **clear the error buffer right before transitioning to $N=3$**. This ensures that the baseline represents the *asymptotic* performance of the trained $N=2$ model, making the threshold much more sensitive to the $N=3$ environment complexity shift.
2. **Tune Covariance Regularization Weight:** Increase the weight of the covariance loss ($\text{cov\_weight}$) from $1.0$ to $10.0$ or $25.0$ to actively penalize high dimension cross-correlation and force orthogonal representation subspaces.
3. **Decouple Pre-training and Adaptation:** Introduce a warmup or stabilization period where the covariance regularizer is dominating, preventing early-stage collapse.
