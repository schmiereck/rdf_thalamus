### Context & Research Direction
In Phase 6, we discovered that while the Dynamic JEPA (GDASR) successfully recruits a 4th representation dimension (80% recruitment rate) when transitioning from $N=3$ to $N=4$ objects, this newly recruited dimension does not automatically specialize or correlate with the physical position of the new object ($|r| \approx 0.0456$). This confirms that unsupervised temporal surprise minimization alone is insufficient to drive coordinate-aligned specialization in the latent space without downstream task pressures or explicit spatial readouts.

To address this limitation and answer the open questions on whether downstream task performance can drive recruited dimensions to self-organize, Phase 7 will introduce **Downstream Task-Coupled Plasticity**. We will train an auxiliary spatial prediction/localization readout probe and backpropagate its gradient specifically into the newly recruited dimension (utilizing the thalamic gating and plasticity-routing principles), and evaluate whether this downstream feedback forces the recruited dimension to self-organize and align with the physical coordinates of the novel object.
