### Research Plan for Phase 2: Thalamic Gating (Pillar D)

To systematically transition from a stable single-layer representation base (Phase 1) to a hierarchical, selectively plastic network, we focus on **Thalamic Gating** (Pillar D). The core challenges in hierarchical representation learning without decoders are **input-drift collapse** (where continuous changes in lower-layer representations prevent upper-layer convergence) and **attentional routing stability**. 

We propose a two-layer hierarchical network where learning is mediated by a surprise-based attention watchdog. Plasticity is gated such that only the layer holding the Attention Token updates its weights, while the non-attended layer runs in inference-only mode. To stabilize inter-layer coupling, we introduce a **Stability Lock** and a **Token Cooldown**.

### Detailed Methodological Design

#### 1. Cross-Scale Normalization of Surprise
Because L1 and L2 operate on different dimensionalities, spatial scopes, and error characteristics, absolute prediction errors cannot be compared directly. We normalize the local surprise $E_l(t)$ using online running estimates of mean $\mu_l(t)$ and standard deviation $\sigma_l(t)$:
$$\mu_l(t) = (1 - \alpha)\mu_l(t-1) + \alpha E_l(t)$$
$$\sigma_l^2(t) = (1 - \alpha)\sigma_l^2(t-1) + \alpha (E_l(t) - \mu_l(t))^2$$
$$\bar{E}_l(t) = \frac{E_l(t) - \mu_l(t)}{\sigma_l(t) + \epsilon}$$
where $\alpha = 0.01$ and $\epsilon = 10^{-5}$.

#### 2. Plasticity Gating & Optimization
When Layer $l$ holds the Attention Token:
*   Its weights $\theta_l$ are updated using gradients computed from its prediction error and VICReg loss.
*   Its GDASR (dynamic dimension recruitment) module is active.
*   The other layer is set to inference-only mode, stopping all gradient flows and dimension recruitment. This ensures a stable, stationary input coordinate space for the non-active layer, preventing co-adaptation and input-drift collapse.
