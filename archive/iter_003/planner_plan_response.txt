## Detailed Analysis and Experimental Plan

### 1. Analysis of Current State and Prior Results
In Phase 1 of the Thalamus project (executed in `iter_002`), we attempted to validate passive representation learning (Pillars A, B, and C) and Gradient-Directed Aligned Subspace Recruitment (GDASR). The empirical results refuted our initial assumptions:
* **Threshold Inflation:** GDASR failed to recruit any new dimensions (recruitment rate = 0.0) upon the transition to 3 objects. This was because the historical error buffer accumulated extremely large errors from the early random-initialization phase, inflating the threshold and making the system insensitive to the introduction of a new object.
* **Representation Collapse:** Under a VICReg loss with `cov_weight=1.0`, representations collapsed completely to collinear 1D manifolds (mean absolute correlation $r > 0.99$, representation collapse rate = 1.0).

### 2. Proposed Algorithmic Improvements

#### A. Preventing Representation Collapse
To prevent different representation dimensions from encoding redundant information, we must strongly penalize correlation between dimensions. We will increase the VICReg covariance regularization weight (`cov_weight`) to `25.0`. 
Additionally, we introduce a **representation-warmup phase** of 1,000 steps. During this warmup, the representation network learns basic spatial and temporal patterns without attempting any dimension recruitment. This allows the representation coordinates to stabilize and prevent early chaotic weight updates from collapsing the subspace.

#### B. Ensuring Sensitive and Reliable Subspace Recruitment
To resolve threshold inflation, we replace the cumulative error buffer in the GDASR module with a **rolling sliding-window error buffer** of size $W = 500$ steps. 
Furthermore, we clear/reset this buffer immediately after the warmup phase finishes, and right before the environment transitions from 2 objects to 3 objects. This ensures that the baseline error threshold represents the current stabilized predictive capability of the network, making it highly sensitive to the sudden increase in temporal prediction error (surprise) caused by the newly introduced object.

### 3. Falsification and Success Metrics
* **Stabilization success:** Mean absolute cross-dimension correlation $r < 0.30$.
* **Sensitivity success:** Dynamic recruitment rate $> 80\%$ (at least 4 out of 5 seeds successfully recruit a dimension upon N=3 transition).
* **Representational efficiency:** Final temporal prediction simulation loss is kept close to or below the baseline B1 ($E \le 0.080$).
