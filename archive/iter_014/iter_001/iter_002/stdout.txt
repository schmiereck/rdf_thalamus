## Lazy-encoder analysis

By the pre-registered definition (`std_vel_3 < 1.5` AND `mse_cent ≥ 70.0`), no arm triggers the failure mode at the seed-aggregated level. However, the velocity metric reveals a deeper structural observation:

- **All three arms — including the un-regularized Arm G baseline — produce `std_vel_3 ≈ 0.04–0.06`**, i.e. four orders of magnitude below the 1.5 threshold. The novel-object centroid in the evaluation sequence is effectively stationary in latent space across all arms.
- Arm J actually has the **highest** velocity (0.060) yet the **worst** MSE (65.7). Arm K has the **lowest** velocity (0.041) and the **best** MSE (62.6). Velocity therefore does not predict accuracy in this regime.
- Implication: the chosen velocity threshold of 1.5 pixels/frame is calibrated against a fundamentally different velocity regime than what the offline evaluation env produces (pointer stays static; novel-object latent drift is naturally small). This makes the metric non-discriminative as deployed, and indicates the threshold should be re-grounded — perhaps in the *normalized* coordinate space ([0, 1]) rather than pixel space, or contextualized by a baseline velocity ratio.

## Per-seed variability dominates the signal

| Seed | Arm G | Arm J | Arm K |
|---|---|---|---|
| 42  | 68.4  | 71.5  | 64.2 |
| 123 | 79.8  | 76.1  | 78.3 |
| 456 | 107.9 | 110.2 | 109.4 |
| 789 | 36.1  | 36.7  | 36.1 |
| 999 | 30.6  | 33.8  | **25.1** |

Seed 456 fails catastrophically across all arms (MSE ~110, soft var ~10), and seeds 789 and 999 are uniformly strong (MSE 25–37). The cross-seed range (25 → 110) dwarfs the cross-arm differences (≤3 MSE units within any seed). CCR neither rescues the bad seeds nor hurts the good ones — its effect is dominated by seed-determined initialization and environment trajectory.

## Scientific interpretation

1. **Numerically, the CCR-Covariance bottleneck regularizer (Arm K) is the best variant on MSE**, marginally beating both the baseline and the hinge formulation. The hinge variant is uniformly the weakest — its margin-based pairwise penalty appears to fight the predictive objective rather than complement it, evidenced by a near-2× increase in test_sim_loss with no MSE improvement.
2. **The hypothesis is falsified by Criterion 2**, but with an important caveat: the baseline itself (0.084) already exceeds the 0.050 sim-loss threshold. Criterion 2 was therefore unachievable a priori by *any* arm under the current passive-pretraining + perturbed-N=4 active phase. This is a flaw in threshold calibration, not evidence that CCR degrades prediction.
3. **CCR does not statistically alter post-collision predictive integrity** (Welch's p ≥ 0.22). Arm K is essentially indistinguishable from baseline; Arm J trends worse but lacks significance with N=5.
4. **No lazy-encoder collapse is detected** under the formal definition, but the velocity diagnostic is uninformative as currently calibrated — every arm sits four orders of magnitude below threshold. This is itself a notable diagnostic finding: the offline test env produces a near-stationary latent trace for channel 3, so absolute MSE (a bias measure) is the dominant signal, not velocity (a variance measure).
5. **Practical recommendation for follow-up**: (a) recalibrate Criterion 2's threshold from a baseline measurement (e.g., baseline +20%); (b) re-ground the lazy-encoder threshold in normalized-coordinate space or as a velocity ratio versus a moving-object reference; (c) the CCR-Covariance loss is worth keeping as a candidate regularizer (it shows the best MSE), but it does not deliver a transformative improvement on the present benchmark.
