## Scientific Justification & Architectural Alignment

In Phase 14, the introduction of Contrastive Coordinate Regularization (CCR) via soft covariance-based constraints (Arm K) successfully resolved the active-perception coordinate drift problem. However, applying a static regularization strength ($\lambda_{cov}$) across all steps introduces a trade-off:
- During stable physical trajectories (low surprise), the spatial bottleneck requires less constraint as the dynamics are already well-modeled.
- During high-velocity collisions or transitions (high surprise), the prediction gradient spikes enormously, dominating the optimization landscape and causing the spatial bottleneck to drift temporarily (known as tracking lag or slip).

By introducing **Surprise-Adaptive Contrastive Coordinate Regularization (SA-CCR)**, we directly address this trade-off. Proportional scaling (Arm L) dynamically tightens the spatial bottleneck constraint when prediction error spikes, shielding the coordinate representations from gradient washouts during chaotic collisions. Conversely, inverse scaling (Arm M) relaxes constraints during high-surprise events to allow maximum representational flexibility. 

Comparing these two dynamic regimes against the fixed-weight baseline (Arm K) provides a highly rigorous, falsifiable scientific test of how surprise-driven gating can modulate representation-level constraints in real-time.
