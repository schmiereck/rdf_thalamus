## Detailed Plan & Research Justification

### 1. Context and the "Spatial Specialization Gap"
In Phase 9, we developed a Dynamic Surprise-Modulated Spatial Bottleneck Curriculum (DSMC). It was highly successful in navigating environmental phase transitions ($N=3 \to N=4$ objects) by dropping the spatial regularization penalty $\lambda(t)$ to zero during surprise spikes to allow representation recruitment, and ramping it back up as predictability returned to compress the representations. 

However, we uncovered a fundamental trade-off: **Spatial localization always incurs a minor predictive capacity cost.** Forcing a single latent channel to compress its representation into a tight spatial centroid discarded non-spatial, high-frequency temporal details needed for accurate simulation predictions. This caused the DSMC curriculum (Arm C) to suffer a 22.8% increase in test simulation loss compared to the unconstrained Gentle Bottleneck (Arm A).

### 2. The Solution: Dual-Stream Decoupling
To resolve this trade-off, we propose separating the latent representation of each node into two functional streams:
*   **The Spatial Coordinate Stream ($z^{coord}$):** A low-dimensional (1D) channel focused entirely on representing "where" an entity is. It is subject to strict spatial variance minimization, yielding a highly localized spatial centroid.
*   **The Temporal Dynamics Stream ($z^{dyn}$):** A multi-dimensional latent channel focused on representing "what" the entity is doing (e.g., velocity, shape features, or internal state). It is free from the spatial variance penalty, preventing representation distortion.

By coupling them such that the dynamics stream can predict future spatial coordinates without the coordinate stream's spatial layout being disrupted by backpropagated temporal error gradients (via stop-gradients), we expect to achieve both **perfect spatial localization** and **exceptional temporal prediction accuracy**.
