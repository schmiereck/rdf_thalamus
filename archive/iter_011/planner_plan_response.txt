### Analysis of Current State & Failure Modes
In Phase 10, the Dual-Stream Decoupled Thalamus (DSDT, Arm D) attempted to bypass the regularization-prediction trade-off by completely isolating the spatial coordinate stream $z^{coord}$ from prediction gradients. This triggered a catastrophic failure: without prediction or visual gradients, $z^{coord}$ suffered from **semantic blindness**, failing to ground to actual physical objects. This resulted in a 100% collapse rate, extremely high spatial variance (1356.52), and high prediction loss (13.44).

To resolve this, we propose **Progressive Decoupling with Representational Consolidation (PDRC, Arm E)**. By initially training both streams jointly, the coordinate stream is forced to ground itself on physical features to minimize prediction error. Once grounded, its weights are consolidated (frozen) to prevent drift, and stop-gradients are introduced to protect the spatial bottleneck from being diluted by subsequent dynamics learning.

### Experimental Protocol

- **Arm D (Control)**: Immediate decoupling (DSDT from Phase 10).
- **Arm E (Proposed)**: PDRC with $T_{ground}$ set to 50% of the total training epochs.
