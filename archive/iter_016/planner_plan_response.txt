### Analysis of the Current State and Problem Context
In Phase 15, we discovered a major bottleneck in the structural "Dual Control" architecture (Arm N). The Minimum Description Length (MDL) gate designed to prevent chaotic dimensionality growth suffered from severe **cold-start predictor bias**. When a new dimension was proposed, its newly initialized ("cold") predictor head exhibited extremely high validation prediction loss on the consistency buffer, resulting in consistency ratios (`L_consistency`) of $1.3 \times 10^4$ to $3.4 \times 10^4$. Consequently, the gate rejected 100% of recruitment proposals, leaving the model stuck at 3 dimensions and unable to track the novel 4th object.

### The Proposed Solution: WUP-PVU Gating
To address this bias without compromising the core "surprise-driven" philosophy of the Thalamus architecture, we propose two key modifications:
1. **Probationary Warm-Up Period (WUP):** Instead of immediately evaluating the proposed dimension, we place it on a "probation" of $W = 100$ steps. During this time, its encoder projection and predictor head are updated, allowing the predictor to transition from "cold" to "warm" before evaluation.
2. **Predictability-Variance-Uniqueness (PVU) Gating:** Rather than comparing total prediction losses (which is mathematically biased when changing dimensionality), we evaluate the new dimension on its own merits:
   - **Non-collapse:** Ensure it represents a dynamic feature rather than a constant.
   - **Predictability:** Ensure its temporal prediction error is significantly lower than its variance (unexplained variance ratio $U_{new} < 0.5$).
   - **Uniqueness:** Ensure it does not duplicate existing dimensions (correlation with existing dimensions $< 0.8$).

By comparing these refined gating strategies against the established baseline (Arm K) and the failed immediate MDL gate (Arm N), we will establish a highly stable, mathematically sound, and biologically inspired mechanism for self-regulated architectural growth.
